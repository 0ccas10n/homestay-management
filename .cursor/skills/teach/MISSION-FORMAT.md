# MISSION.md Format

The `MISSION.md` captures the **why** behind the user's learning. Every lesson should be grounded in this mission. Without it, teaching becomes abstract.

## When to Write

Write `MISSION.md` at the **start** of every new learning journey. Confirm the content with the user before saving.

## Template

```markdown
# Mission: [Topic]

**Learner**: [Name or handle]
**Started**: [YYYY-MM-DD]
**Last updated**: [YYYY-MM-DD]
**Status**: [Active | Paused | Completed]

## Why this matters

A short paragraph (3–5 sentences) written in the learner's voice. Capture:
- The concrete real-world outcome they want
- Why now (what triggered the interest)
- Who else is affected by this learning

## What I want to be able to do

A numbered list of **observable, concrete capabilities**. Each item should be testable:
- ✅ "Read a weather map and explain what's coming"
- ❌ "Understand meteorology"

Aim for 3–8 capabilities. Order from foundational → aspirational.

## What I already know

A brief inventory of the learner's current level. Include:
- Concepts they already grasp
- Skills they have practiced
- Resources they have already used

This sets the floor for the zone of proximal development.

## Constraints

- Time available per week
- Budget for paid courses / books
- Tools they have or don't have
- Languages they want output in
- Topics they want to avoid

## Definition of success

What does "done" look like? A specific moment in time when they can look back and say "yes, this worked." Concrete, dated if possible.
```

## Updating the Mission

The mission can change as the learner develops. When updating:
1. Add a `## Mission evolution` section at the bottom with dated entries
2. Write a corresponding `learning-record` capturing the change
3. Confirm the change with the user before saving
